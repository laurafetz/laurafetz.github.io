  • Analysed millions of student responses across six maths and language games on an adaptive learning platform used by over 300,000 children.
  • Built workflows to extract, merge and model learning data with multilevel models.
  • Showed that learning differed substantially between individual students, and that focused practice helped in some games but not others.
  • Presented findings and recommendations for the learning-goal algorithm to the Content & Psychometrics team, and contributed to the analysis of a newly launched deadline feature.
Did the sevens stick?

At Prowise, both questions met in the classroom: did children really learn, and would the scores show it? Normal practice mixes it up: 5 × 7, 3 × 2, 7 × 9. But sometimes students get a focused learning-goal session with only the 7 times table. Using multilevel models on large learning datasets, I tested whether students actually got better afterwards.

2021–2022

Research AssistantLudwig-Maximilians University of Munich

Where it all started.

My first research job meant sitting on tiny chairs across from four- to seven-year-olds, running reasoning tasks and listening to how they explained their answers. I collected the data, transcribed every conversation, and handled the admin that keeps a study running. It's where I learned that behind every data point is a child with their own idea of what the question meant.

A 2026 study involving Köksal and Sodian linked early understanding of other people’s minds with later scientific reasoning. This wider research asks how social understanding and thinking about evidence develop together.

2023–2026

Research Intern · Research AssistantARQ & University of Amsterdam

One child at a time.

At ARQ, I analysed single-case data from family-based interventions designed to keep children safe, following each family closely, session after session. At the same time, I joined a research team at the University of Amsterdam, collecting data from published single-case studies for a meta-analysis. What began as data collection became our 2026 paper, with me as joint first author. It's where I got hooked on the question I still ask: what works on average, and what works for this one child?

Marija Maric and colleagues’ 2023 meta-analysis examines interventions for internalizing disorders in children and adolescents, including anxiety, depression, and post-traumatic stress. It shows how single-case data can be brought together to explore individual change and patterns across studies.

2024–2025

Master’s thesis · Junior ResearcherUniversity of Amsterdam

What if g isn't a thing?

Tracking change one child at a time made me wonder what our scores actually capture. For decades, intelligence has been explained by one hidden general factor, g. My master's thesis asked whether a web of abilities that strengthen each other could explain the same data just as well. As a junior researcher, I extended it with simulations, testing when g truly fits and when it only looks like it does.

Explore the direction

Related simulation research tests how competing models can account for similar patterns. It encourages researchers to consider network explanations alongside factor models. The bigger question: when does a well-fitting model actually help explain the process behind the data?
